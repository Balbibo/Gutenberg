Mountain Paths

Mountain Paths is a collection of eighteen essays by Belgian writer Maurice Maeterlinck, translated into English by Alexander Teixeira de Mattos. The book was originally published in the United States by Dodd, Mead and Company in 1919.

The essays explore a wide range of subjects, but many return to questions about life, death, the human spirit, memory, morality, nature, and the meaning of existence. Maeterlinck approaches these subjects philosophically, often moving between everyday observations and much larger questions about humanity and the universe.

The collection opens with “The Power of the Dead,” an essay about the continuing influence of people who have died and the way they remain present through our memories, thoughts, and actions. Other essays consider spiritual communication, the effects of war, society, nature, human behaviour, progress, heredity, hope, and karma.

The author

Maurice Maeterlinck (1862–1949) was a Belgian writer whose work included essays, plays, poetry, and philosophical writings. Mountain Paths belongs to his essays and sits alongside works such as The Treasure of the Humble, Wisdom and Destiny, The Life of the Bee, Our Eternity, and The Unknown Guest. Project Gutenberg Maeterlinck's writing frequently explores questions that sit between philosophy, spirituality, nature, and human experience. Mountain Paths is particularly interested in the unseen forces that shape our lives and in the relationship between the individual, society, nature, and the wider universe.
